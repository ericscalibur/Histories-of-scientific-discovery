# Histories of Scientific Discovery

Interactive story-lessons for kids (~age 10) that teach math through the true, dramatic
history of astronomy — comets that broke the sky, plague-year breakthroughs, heartbreak
expeditions, pigeon-related Nobel Prizes.

Each lesson is a **single self-contained HTML file**: no server, no internet, no
installation. Double-click it, and the story unfolds chapter by chapter — but each
chapter is locked behind a **math checkpoint** built from the real numbers of the real
history (Tycho Brahe's actual parallax figures, Newton's actual Moon test, the actual
1919 eclipse measurements). Solve the problem, unlock the next chapter, and light
another star on the progress tracker. Finish everything and the lesson issues a
certificate in the student's name.

## The lessons

| Lesson | Era | Checkpoints | Math highlights |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Treasure of the Sky** — Tycho Brahe & Johannes Kepler | 1546–1627 | 17 | Parallax calculations, arcminutes, Kepler's three laws, the Cassini–Richer "measure the solar system" capstone |
| **The Apple and the Moon** — Isaac Newton | 1642–1727 | 10 | The 60² Moon test, inverse-square law, Newton's three laws with problems |
| **The Man Who Measured the World** — Eratosthenes | ~240 BC | 5 | Angles, proportions, measuring Earth with a stick and a shadow |
| **The Day the Stars Moved** — Einstein & the 1919 eclipse | 1915–1919 | 6 | Arcseconds, predicted vs. measured, fractions |
| **The Unluckiest Astronomer** — Le Gentil & the transit of Venus | 1761–1769 | 6 | Parallax at planetary scale, elapsed time, the first global science project |
| **The Hiss from the Beginning of Time** — the "pigeon poop" Nobel | 1964 | 5 | Negative temperatures, unit conversion, big numbers |
| **The Yardstick of the Universe** — Henrietta Leavitt | 1893–1923 | 6 | Reading patterns in data, rates, the cosmic distance ladder |

Plus **constellation plotting worksheets** (`worksheets/`): 12 real constellations as
(x, y) coordinate connect-the-dots on a four-quadrant grid, built from J2000 star
catalog positions — answer keys included.

## Using the lessons

Download any file from `lessons/` and open it in a browser. That's it. Progress lives
in the page (refreshing restarts the lesson — by design, for classroom re-use).

The Tycho & Kepler lesson is the flagship: on a wide screen its margins hold real
historical artifacts (Tycho's own 1573 nova map, the 1598 mural quadrant plate, the
Rudolphine Tables frontispiece, period comet woodcuts). **Hover an image** and a
parchment scroll unrolls with the story behind the artifact; **click** to open it
full-size.

## Principles

1. **Real history, honestly told.** Every date, quote, and number is checked. Where a
   famous story is myth (the apple bonking Newton's head), the lesson says so — and
   where an image is commonly mislabeled online (a certain 1618 comet engraving),
   catching the mislabel *is* the lesson.
2. **Real artifacts only.** Illustrations are photographs of actual historical objects
   and period engravings — never AI-generated imagery.
3. **The math is the history.** Checkpoints use the numbers the scientists actually
   worked with: 60 arcminutes of lunar parallax, 8 arcminutes of Mars error, 60² for
   the Moon test.
4. **Recurring themes:** evidence over comfortable belief; measure carefully enough
   that no one can argue; truth over authority; share your data; check every caption.

## Building from source

Six of the lessons are generated from data files by a small Python engine:

```bash
cd src
python3 build_all.py     # writes into ../lessons/
```

`Tycho_and_Kepler_Interactive_Story.html` is the hand-maintained reference
implementation (margin artifacts, scroll animations) and is **not** regenerated by the
build — treat it as source in its own right.

Tests (requires Node + Playwright + Chromium): `src/tests/` contains full
click-through tests that solve every checkpoint and verify chapter unlocking, the star
tracker, and the certificate.
