# Interactive Astronomy Story Ideas — Planning Doc

Companion series to the Constellation Plotting Worksheets. Each story = one
self-contained interactive HTML file: chapters unlock by solving math
checkpoints (10-year-old level), progress star tracker, hints after two misses,
certificate with the student's name at the end.

## Full idea bank

### Directly continues the Tycho & Kepler threads
- **Galileo and the Telescope Trial (1609–1642)** — moons around Jupiter; the
  Inquisition; faith vs. evidence continued. *Math: timing Jupiter's moons,
  magnification ratios.*
- **Halley's Comet (1705)** — predicting a comet's return 53 years after your
  own death, and being right. *Math: orbital periods, return-year prediction.*
- **Neptune, the Planet Found with a Pen (1846)** — Le Verrier computes an
  unseen planet's position; found within 1° on the first night. *Math:
  prediction vs. observation, degrees on the sky.*

### Great expedition dramas
- **Transit of Venus / Le Gentil (1761–69)** — in queue (#4).
- **1919 Eclipse / Eddington & Einstein** — in queue (#3). Note: Einstein was
  alive — this made him a celebrity. (Not to be confused with the Venus
  transits!)
- **The Longitude Prize (1714–1773)** — carpenter John Harrison vs. the
  astronomy establishment; 40 years of perfect clocks. *Math: 15° per hour,
  time zones, clock arithmetic.*

### Ancient & foundational
- **Eratosthenes (~240 BC)** — in queue (#2). Best pure-math story ever.
- **Ole Rømer Clocks the Speed of Light (1676)** — Jupiter's moons run late
  when Earth is far away. *Math: distance = speed × time.*

### The women who measured the universe
- **Henrietta Leavitt & the Harvard Computers** — in queue (#6).
- **Cecilia Payne (1925)** — proves stars are hydrogen; told to soften her
  conclusion; vindicated. Echoes Kepler: truth vs. authority. *Math: element
  proportions, percentages.*
- **Jocelyn Bell Burnell & the "Little Green Men" (1967)** — grad student
  finds pulsars; supervisor gets the Nobel. *Math: pulse timing, decimals.*

### Pure kid-appeal gold
- **The Pigeon Poop Nobel Prize (1964)** — in queue (#5).
- **The Farm Boy Who Found Pluto (1930)** — Clyde Tombaugh, tractor-part
  telescopes, blink comparator; bonus: Pluto's reclassification as
  changing-your-mind-on-evidence. *Math: motion between dated images.*
- **The Asteroid That Ended the Dinosaurs (1980)** — Alvarez father & son,
  the iridium layer, the Chicxulub crater. *Math: crater scale, layers as
  timelines.*
- **Katherine Johnson & the Human Computers (1961–69)** — John Glenn: "get
  the girl to check the numbers." Kepler's ellipses with astronauts aboard.
  *Math: verifying calculations, orbits, mission elapsed time.*

## Recurring themes to keep harping on
1. Change your beliefs based on observation, not on what you want to be true.
2. Measure carefully enough that no one can argue.
3. Truth over comfort — and over authority (church, establishment, or your
   own beautiful theory).
4. Share your data; hoarded knowledge dies.
5. Wonder is where science starts — often in a kid looking up.
