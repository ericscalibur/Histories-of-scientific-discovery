✦ The Spectroscope Bench ✦

Make gases glow, shine light through them, and read the lines they leave in the rainbow

How to use the bench. Drag a light source or a gas tube from the rack into the holder at the back of the bench. Its light passes through the two holders in the beam, then through a slit and a prism, and spreads out into the spectrum below. Put more tubes in the beam to see what each gas does to the light behind it. (You can also click an item, then click a holder.)

Each tube has an exciter: a high-voltage supply that makes the gas inside glow. Switch it off and the gas goes cool and dark. Every lamp and tube on the bench has its own slider: Heat for the limelight, Brightness for the Sun and the candle, Glow for a tube.

Kirchhoff’s rule (1859). A glowing gas gives bright lines at its own colours. Put it in front of a brighter light and the same lines turn dark, because the gas soaks up more of those colours than it gives out. Put it in front of a dimmer light and they stay bright. Each element always uses the same lines, like a fingerprint.
Line positions are measured wavelengths in nanometres (nm). Brightnesses are simplified so that they show up well on a screen.
Real lines are much thinner than drawn here; use the magnifier to see close pairs such as sodium’s.
